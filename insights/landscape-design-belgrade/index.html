Landscape design guide

Compose the environment, not the decoration

A convincing outdoor space is not a collection of attractive plants placed around architecture. It is a composition of movement, proportion, planting, material and time. In Belgrade, that composition also has to respond to real exposure, maintenance and seasonal conditions.

01

Read the site before choosing plants

The first decisions come from the site itself: orientation, sun and shade, soil, drainage, existing vegetation, access, circulation and the relationship to the building. Plant selection comes later because it needs a spatial role, not just a visual preference.

02

Movement and sightlines create the underlying structure

Paths, entries, focal points and visual axes decide how a space is experienced. A strong layout makes movement feel obvious without becoming mechanical. It also creates places where planting can act as frame, screen, rhythm or emphasis.

03

Planting should operate as a living system

Planting changes through the year and over many years. A useful strategy considers mature size, succession, maintenance, texture, seasonality and compatibility. The objective is a composition that develops rather than one that looks finished only on installation day.

04

Color works best when contrast is controlled

Expressive color can be powerful, but too many competing accents weaken spatial clarity. Controlled contrast allows selected moments to carry intensity while the rest of the planting provides structure, rhythm and depth.

05

Implementation is part of design quality

A concept becomes real through dimensions, material choices, sourcing, planting quality and on-site decisions. The gap between a drawing and a built environment is where many projects lose coherence, so implementation needs the same level of attention as the concept.
